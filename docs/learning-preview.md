# Three-format industry learning

The homepage now surfaces Water Infrastructure with Quick Brief, Full Primer
and Interview Prep entry points, recruiting-interest and availability filters.
`content/water-infrastructure.json` is the shared editorial source for all three
formats. Run `python scripts/build_learning.py` for HTML and add `--word` to
regenerate the matching Word edition. Original downloads and readers remain.

Six primary sources were checked on 2026-11-06. Inline references expose the
supported assertion, short evidence excerpt, source date, review date and
section dependencies. Worked calculations and investment frameworks are
explicitly analytical. This is a scoped U.S. water economics edition, not a
claim that every legacy fact was reverified or that autonomous verification
is operational. Other industries still have original readers only.

The static page has accessible keyboard tabs, section links, expandable model
answers and evidence drilldowns. All content remains visible without JavaScript.
The Word edition is rendered by CI and checked for the existing 10-page and
11pt requirements. Actual rendered pages must also be visually inspected.

The monitor consumes source dependencies from this editorial source. It can
identify sections due for review; paid refresh and autonomous publishing remain
disabled. Homepage recruiting filters are editorial category groupings, not
employer recommendations. No quizzes, accounts or personalized features added.
## Reading-first revision
Briefs target approximately five minutes (700–1,100 words), per the user's
latest direction. Use short labeled sections, practical examples and clear
spacing rather than dense continuous prose. Sources and archival context stay
collapsed; full-primer and Word content are unchanged. Interview answers open
one at a time. The Water brief expands the existing source-backed economics
and hypothetical examples without introducing new market claims.

## Explanatory visuals
The Water brief uses a simplified service-chain diagram and a hypothetical
debt-coverage comparison. Both use semantic HTML with visible text, require no
interaction, reflow for phones, and retain labels in print. Cash bars share
one scale; patterns and labels supplement color. The accompanying prose
explains interpretation rather than repeating each plotted number. Full
primer and Word content remain aligned with their existing source material.

## Teaching sequence
The brief explains the service and three business models before introducing
investment economics. One hypothetical treatment-plant upgrade connects roles,
construction funding, customer payments and the debt-coverage visual. Detailed
rate-base and payback calculations remain in the full primer. Interview
questions are grouped into industry basics, business economics and investment
judgment, each with a direct answer before the supporting reasoning.
